## 🌍 Overview

The Adaptive Environmental Enclosure is a controlled micro-environment designed to improve the reliability, efficiency and operational lifespan of electrical and electronic equipment operating in the High Altitude Areas (HAA) and Super High Altitude Areas (SHAA) of Ladakh.

Extreme cold, low atmospheric pressure, reduced air density, thermal cycling and increased UV exposure can affect the performance and reliability of electrical and electronic systems. The proposed system protects existing equipment by controlling its surrounding environment rather than modifying the equipment itself.

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Electrical and electronic equipment operating in high-altitude regions such as Ladakh can experience:

- Reduced cooling efficiency due to low air density
- Increased risk of electrical arcing under low-pressure conditions
- Battery performance degradation in extreme cold
- Thermal cycling caused by large temperature variations
- Increased exposure to UV radiation

These conditions can reduce equipment reliability, efficiency and operational lifespan.

## 💡 Proposed Solution

The proposed solution is a sealed and UV-protected enclosure that creates a controlled micro-environment around existing electrical and electronic equipment.

The system automatically regulates internal pressure and temperature while using a sequential cooling architecture to manage heat generated by the protected equipment.

The design does not require modification of the existing hardware or software.

## ⚙️ System Architecture

The system consists of:

- STM32 microcontroller
- Internal and external pressure sensors
- Internal and external temperature sensors
- Pressure pump/compressor
- Safety/relief valve
- Heating element
- Inner coolant loop
- External A/B/C cooling stages
- Coolant pump
- Control valves
- UV-protected enclosure

The STM32 processes sensor feedback and controls the pressure, heating and cooling systems automatically.

## 🌡️ Thermal Management

The thermal management system uses an inner coolant loop and three external cooling stages.

**Thermal flow:**

Inner Coolant Loop → Coolant Pump → Cooling Stage A → Cooling Stage B → Cooling Stage C → Return to Inner Coolant Loop

The inner coolant loop absorbs heat generated by the protected equipment. The external A/B/C cooling stages operate sequentially to reject heat to the cold external environment.

This architecture provides continuous heat management while making effective use of the low external temperature.

## 💨 Pressure Control

The pressure-control system maintains a controlled internal environment.

**Pressure-control flow:**

Pressure Sensor → STM32 → Pressure Pump → Safety/Relief Valve → Feedback

The pressure sensor continuously monitors the internal condition. The STM32 processes the sensor feedback and controls the pressure pump accordingly.

A safety/relief valve provides an additional protection mechanism against excessive internal pressure.

## 🔄 Closed-Loop Control

The complete system operates using closed-loop control.

**Control cycle:**

Sensor Measurement → STM32 Processing → Actuator Control → Re-measurement

The STM32 continuously receives sensor data and automatically adjusts the pressure pump, heating element, coolant pump and control valves according to the required operating conditions.

## Existing Approaches and Proposed System

| Existing approach | Main approach | Limitation in HAA/SHAA |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional equipment enclosure | Basic physical protection | Does not actively regulate the internal environment |
| Equipment-level thermal modification | Modifies or optimizes individual equipment | Requires changes to existing equipment |
| Active heating systems | Maintains equipment temperature | Does not address pressure and cooling together |
| Conventional cooling systems | Removes equipment heat | Cooling efficiency can be affected by low-density air |
| Hawk165 proposed system | Controlled pressure + temperature + sequential cooling | Provides an integrated environmental-control approach |

## 🌟 Uniqueness

- No modification of existing hardware or software.
- Sequential A/B/C cooling architecture.
- Controlled micro-environment instead of modifying the protected equipment.
- Automated pressure and temperature regulation using closed-loop control.
- Modular enclosure concept that can be adapted for different electrical and electronic systems.

## ⚠️ Challenges & Limitations

- Increased enclosure size and weight.
- Pressure safety requires engineering validation.
- Thermal and pressure performance require physical testing.
- Actual high-altitude performance requires environmental testing.
- Final enclosure dimensions and pressure ratings require detailed mechanical analysis.
